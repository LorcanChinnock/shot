# Recording

Read this before changing screen recording, pausing or audio.

- `Recorder` uses `SCStream` + `SCRecordingOutput`.
- Each pause starts a new segment, and `VideoConcatenator` joins them on stop.
- With both the microphone and system audio on, each is also written to its own file, so mute silences only the microphone.
- The clock, mute stretches and segment lists live in ShotCore (`RecordingTimeline`, `RecordingSegments`), so `Recorder` only drives SCK and the panels.
- The camera bubble shows, and the recording starts, only once `CameraWarmUp` has seen the camera's first frames with its exposure settled (or 1.5 s pass), so a recording never opens on black frames.
- `Recorder.Options` holds this recording's choices: camera, microphone, cursor, Mac sound and frame rate. They start from Settings, and the check-before-recording bar can change them. While that bar shows the microphone on, `DevicePreview.microphone` runs it for the level meter, and it stops before the recording opens the microphone.
- `RecordingSetupController` picks the area through the same overlay as a screenshot, then shows the check-before-recording bar (`SetupBarView`) or the shared countdown panel. `RecordingStart.after` in ShotCore decides which: the bar shows when Settings asks for it or ⌥ was held as the pick ended. `RecordSetupFrame` in ShotCore lays out the dim around the frame and writes its size chip, and `LevelBars` works out how many of the microphone's bars are lit, with tests.
- The controls are `RecordingControlPanel`, a floating panel that never takes the keyboard. Its dot breathes over `Moment.recordingPulse` only while recording. Red goes while paused: Resume becomes the one ink button, in the accent, and Stop turns secondary.
- `RecordingSessionModel.seconds` is the timer the controls and the menu bar show. Its clock ticks once on each whole second recorded, from `showPanels` to `hidePanels`, so neither redraws more often. `RecordingClock` in ShotCore formats it. `Recorder.onSecond` passes it to `AppState.recordingSeconds`, and `StatusMenu` then draws `BrandMark.timerImage` in place of the mark.
- The border (`RecordingBorderPanel`) sits outside the recorded area and is left out of the video like Shot's other windows. Shape layers draw it, so a large area costs no bitmap. While paused it's dashed in amber with no glow, since red means recording.
- With Show clicks on, `ClickRings` draws Shot's own ring for each click inside the area, in one small panel that the filter lets in, so the ring is in the video. Event monitors catch the clicks, which needs no permission, and a click while paused gets no ring. `makeFilter` fetches the shareable content again until it lists that panel, since the window server can take a moment to list a window just ordered in. A window recording captures only its window, so there SCK's `showMouseClicks` draws the clicks instead. `RecordingBorder` and `ClickRing` in ShotCore hold the sizes and timing, with tests.
- `CameraBubble` is one panel just larger than the circle, with macOS's own window shadow, which follows the circle and catches no clicks. The filter lets it in, so the bubble and its shadow are in the video. While checking before recording, `showsHandle` adds the resize handle; `setSetupVisible` turns it off for the countdown and the recording. `CameraBubbleStyle` and `CameraBubbleLayout` in ShotCore hold its sizes, where the handle sits and which size a handle drag asks for, with tests.
- Discard keeps the segments until the toast goes: `complete(discard:)` hands `Toast` a `PendingAction` whose undo joins them as Stop would, and whose commit deletes them. `Toast.commitPending()` runs at quit, so a discard isn't offered back at the next launch.
- At launch, `recoverLeftoverSegments()` sweeps what `SegmentRecovery.plan` can't use and the segments that don't play, then shows `RecordingRecoveryDialog` for the rest. `SegmentRecovery.Leftover` in ShotCore holds the parts with their total length, with tests. Recover runs `Recorder.recover(_:)`, Discard recording deletes the parts behind the same undo toast, whose undo shows the dialog again, and Esc leaves them for the next launch.
