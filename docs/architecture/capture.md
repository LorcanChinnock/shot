# Capture

Read this before changing how Shot takes a screenshot or picks an area, window or display.

- `AppDelegate` owns `AppState` and the `CaptureCoordinator`, wires hotkeys and controller callbacks to it, and routes `shot://` URLs. `ShotApp` is an empty `Settings` scene with an `AppDelegate` adaptor. The menu bar icon and its native menu are `MenuBar/StatusMenu.swift`, an AppKit `NSStatusItem`, so the menu can hold the Recent row (`RecentRowView`). `RecentCapturesStore` decodes its thumbnails off the main thread at launch and each time the menu opens, and `RecentCaptures` in ShotCore picks the four to show. Other long-lived controllers are `.shared` singletons.
- `CaptureCoordinator.perform(_: ShotAction)` is the central dispatcher. `ShotAction` and `EditorRoute` live in ShotCore.
- For an area or window pick, `DisplayCapturer` freezes every display, then `SelectionOverlayController.select` returns an area, window or display.
- The overlay shows one small bar (#1075): Screenshot or Record, the modes Area, Window, Full Screen and Browser (`CaptureBarMode` in ShotCore; Browser is a window pick of just the page, and `B` switches to it or back, overriding the Settings choice for that overlay only; Tab steps through Area, Window and Full Screen, and Space switches between Area and Window), then Delay `D` and Scroll `S`, then Magnifier `M`, Crosshair `C` and Size `I`, which override Settings for that overlay only. The picked mode is the overlay's one ink control, in the accent with an ink outline and drop, and a toggle that's on gets a light fill instead. `CaptureBarLayout` places its buttons and dividers, and each button is a VoiceOver element the overlay draws. The bar fades out (as long as a panel takes to leave, or Reduce Motion's fade) 4 seconds after it appears or the pointer last moved on it, and comes back with any of its hotkeys, so the area under it can be picked at once. It shows only on the screen with the pointer, sits at the bottom, hides while an area is dragged out, and moves to the top when an area made with the arrow keys or the highlighted window or page reaches it, or hides when both edges are in the way. `OverlayBarPlacement` in ShotCore decides, and the bar stays put while the pointer is on it. A button acts on release, so a drag that starts on the bar makes an area. Browser needs Accessibility access to find the page. Without it, picking Browser by click or `B` leaves the overlay in its mode and shows `BrowserPermissionCard` (#1087), its own `FloatingPanelWindow` just above the overlay and next to the bar (`PermissionCardPlacement` in ShotCore). The card takes the keyboard while it shows. Use Window switches to Window, Esc or a click on the overlay closes it, and Grant Access closes the overlay before asking macOS, so the prompt isn't left behind it. The key hints are only in the overlay's VoiceOver help, and the screen dims by just 10% until an area is being made.
- The area being made (#1076) has a white edge with a dark ring, round handles from `AreaHandles`, and the size and ratio chips, which `AreaLabelPlacement` keeps on screen and off the bar. The loupe and the crosshair guides follow the pointer before and while dragging. `Loupe` in ShotCore does the loupe's pixel maths and placement, and `AreaOverlayTests` covers both. The cursor is `SelectionOverlayView.cursor`, the board's two-tone crosshair.
- A highlighted window, page or screen (#1077) gets an accent tint with an accent and ink edge, and a label in its middle with a camera, its name and pixel size, and how to capture it. `WindowPickLabel` in ShotCore lays the label out, dropping parts in a small window and keeping it on screen and off the bar, and `WindowPickTests` covers it. The label is drawn after the bar, since it needs the bar's frame.
- With Delay on, `ScreenshotCountdown` shows the countdown panel (#1078), `CountdownPanel` in `Recording/RecordingSetupController.swift`, which a recording's countdown shares. Its accent ring empties as the seconds run out: `CountdownRing` in ShotCore gives how full it is at each tick, and it drains a second's worth over `Moment.countdownRing`, or stays still under Reduce Motion. The panel orders out 150 ms before the capture, so it's never in the image.
- Pick Colour (#1080) freezes every display with Shot's own windows in, then `ColorPickerOverlay` shows the loupe and returns the pixel picked as sRGB. `ColorLoupe` and `PixelColor` in ShotCore do its layout and colour conversion. Without Screen Recording, `ColorSampler` falls back to the system loupe.
- Fullscreen freezes only its screen and shows no overlay. A picked window is captured again live rather than from the frozen still, and a recording is always live.
- `finish` encodes off the main actor and copies before it saves, so the clipboard is never held up by the file write. `AfterCapture.plan` in ShotCore turns Settings into what happens to the capture, including whether a Quick Access card, the editor or a toast follows. `ViewfinderSnap` plays over the capture once the copy is done, which is why a toast after it shows its tick already drawn.
- Quick Access is `QuickAccess/QuickAccessController.swift` and `QuickAccessView.swift`: a stack of up to five cards in one panel, where the older cards share one place behind the front one and fan out on hover. `QuickAccessLayout` in ShotCore sizes each card from its capture and places the stack. Pin to Screen opens `PinController`, whose bar shows only on hover; `PinLayout` and `PinOpacity` in ShotCore place it and step its opacity. A toast (`Toast.swift`) lines up with the cards' edge and sits above them, as `ToastLayout` in ShotCore works out.
- Scroll is a toggle on the screenshot overlay (`S`, or its button on the bar), off each time. When it is on, the pick (an area, window or screen) goes to `ScrollingCaptureController` instead of being captured, which streams it with `SCStream` while the person scrolls, and `ScrollStitcher` in ShotCore lines each frame up with the last and paints it into a growing canvas. Return hands the image to `finish` like any other capture. The panel (#1086) follows the appearance, unlike the other floating panels. `ScrollPreviewLayout` and `ScrollFrontier` in ShotCore place its preview, and the stitcher's `Update` carries the frames kept and whether a sticky header was found. The stitcher is pure pixel work, tested with synthetic pages in `ScrollStitcherTests`.

## Scroll lab

`scripts/scroll-lab/` checks scrolling capture against real rendering rather than the synthetic pages in `ScrollStitcherTests`. Run it after any change to `ScrollStitcher` or `ScrollCanvas`.

- `run.sh [filter]` renders the scenarios in headless Chrome (pages in `pages.cjs`, scroll paths in `frames.cjs`: wheel notches, flicks, reversals, drops, jumps, sideways and diagonal), replays the frames through `ScrollStitcher` with the `ScrollLab` target, and scores each image against a full-page screenshot. It needs Google Chrome and `puppeteer-core` (installed into `$LAB_DIR`, default `/tmp/shot-scroll-lab`). Frames are cached, so a rerun only costs the stitching. `DPR=1` with another `LAB_DIR` runs the same scenarios as an external, non-Retina display sees them; both scales should pass the same scenarios. `*.diff.png` shows where an image differs (red) and what was never painted (magenta); `CROP=y,height` writes a close-up and `DIAG=<name>` prints each frame's candidates. `VIEW_W` and `VIEW_H` set the view size (default 640 x 440). A move of half a point is drawn by shifting the `list` page's rows, since headless Chrome snaps a scroll offset to a whole point and a real screen moves by device pixels, so odd moves at 2x are covered.
- `live.sh <page> <pattern>` scrolls a real `NSScrollView` (`target.swift`, with overlay scroll bars) using synthetic events (`drive.swift`) while the running `ShotDev` captures it, then scores the saved image. It moves the pointer and sends keys, so leave the machine alone while it runs. The terminal needs Accessibility access.
- `ScrollLab replay <dir> <out.png>` stitches frames saved from a live capture (`f00000.bgra` files with a `meta.txt` of width, height, bytes per row and scale, or `f00000.png` files) and prints how many frames got each status, which tells a live problem in the stitching from one in the final pass (`NOCOMPOSE=1` skips it; `DIAG=1` prints each frame's candidates). The app has no switch that saves frames: to record some, add a temporary write of each `ScrollFrameSink` buffer.
- Sticky columns inset from the edge, as a wiki's contents list and settings panel are, are found as pinned parts (`ScrollBandVotes.blobs`) by what stayed in at least 90% of the votes. When each step is taller than the rows above a pixel, as in Safari where a wheel step jumped 640 device pixels in one frame, those top rows can't vote that they followed the content, so the map takes a "stayed" vote from the reverse test alone (a clear mismatch with what the content from a move on would have brought); rows and columns for bars never do, or a list in a margin would turn everything above it into a bar. Rows that repeat at the move's period, such as a list's lines, are hidden by the same pinned part in every frame, so the composer fills output pixels no frame showed clean from their neighbours in the row. The lab's pages can't show either, since its steps don't match the list's row spacing; `aPinnedListInsideAMargin…` in `ScrollStitcherTests` does, and a replay of recorded frames (`ScrollLab replay`) is how the live case was found.
- A top or bottom band reaches its edge across blank rows and its own rows, not across a stretch of content (more than a gap's eighth of rows that voted as following the page). A floating button near the bottom has the page's text between it and the edge, and its top and bottom edges vote as stayed in every pair, so with only a few moves it was taken as a 58-row bar and the last lines of text were mixed from frames that saw other text in those rows. It is boxed by `ScrollBandVotes.blobs` instead. A translucent bar passes, since its rows from the edge are blank. `aWideFloatingButtonNearTheBottom…` in `ScrollStitcherTests` covers it, and a replay of recorded frames from the `article` scene in `docs/demo` is how it was found.
- The panel's preview is an eighth-size copy of the canvas that is brought up to date where a frame paints new pixels, and in the part it shares with the last frame only where a row was painted over different pixels or over pixels not seen before. Without the first, copies of a button painted as content before it was known to be pinned stayed in it, so the preview showed the button several times while the image had one; without the second, the spot the button had covered stayed hatched after a later frame saw it (`thePanelsPreviewShowsAFloatingButtonOnce…`, which also checks the whole preview against the canvas with `outOfDatePreviewCells`). `ScrollLab replay` writes the preview next to its image as `<out>.preview.png`.
- An overlay scroll bar fades in over the right edge while the content goes past. The composer takes a pixel in the edge strip from the sample nearest the page beside it, which is the pixel at the strip's inner side, or one nearer the edge where content such as a chat bubble reaches into the strip. The last frame replaces the key kept at its position and goes first in the list, since it is the one most likely to be clean and the composer keeps only the first ten samples of a pixel. The end of a long thread in MugCo (`Dana Okafor`) is how this was found, since only the last screenful is seen with the bar over it every time; `aScrollBarThatFadesOnceTheScrollingStopsLeavesNothingAtTheEndOfThePage` covers the first part. Trusting the last frame outright at the edges does not work: a chat's bubbles are 4 pixels narrower once the bar has gone.
- A scenario passes when under 0.6% of its pixels differ, no 24-pixel band is more than 6% wrong, under 0.3% of what a frame could show is missing and under 0.5% is extra. Known failures: steps of more than a third of the area in one frame (150 points on the lab's page, which is a hard flick at 30 frames a second) give up after a few; diagonal scrolling over a pinned column leaves the first digits of the cells beside it clipped; and a bounce on a page whose pinned bars move with it, which a real window may not do, draws those bars again as content near the end.
- `ScrollOvershoot` cuts a rubber-band bounce from the end of the image: a short push past an edge, a return that settles, and flat rows between, still the outermost when the image is read. Nothing is removed while capturing.
