# Design system and views

Read this before changing shared UI components, window chrome or a view in a lazy collection.

- The design system is in `Sources/Shot/Design`, and its values come from [the redesign spec](../redesign/spec.md) (App values). `Theme` in `ThemeStyle.swift` holds the colour tokens (`Theme.window`, `.group`, `.ink`, `.accent`, `.record`, `.selection` and the rest), each with its light and dark value, and `ScaledFont` gives the type styles, which Settings › Text size scales.
- One ink control per screen: the accent fill, the 1.5 pt ink outline and the 2 pt drop go on the action you'll take next, such as Copy, Save, Record or Continue, or on the selected tool. Every other control is quiet. Yellow is the only interface accent, record red shows only while something records or is about to be lost, and selection blue marks selected marks, layers and files.
- Every button is `ThemeButtonStyle(kind)`, with the kind picked by the button's job. `.main` is the one ink button and `.secondary` a white button at the Rest depth. `.plain` is accent text, `.toolbar` is borderless with a hover fill (and `isOn` for a panel that's showing), and `.tool` picks a tool and is drawn in ink while selected. `.popUp` is the button inside `ThemePopUp`. `destructive: true` turns any kind record red, and `large: true` gives onboarding and dialogs more room. Setting the `\.tightToolbar` environment value cuts `.toolbar` and `.tool` padding from 7 to 4 pt either side; the image editor's title bar sets it as its last resort at a larger text size, so Copy stays in the window. Focus is the 4 pt accent halo in place of the system ring.
- Ink is drawn with `inkBorder(_:width:color:)`, or `themeSurface` for a filled shape with ink, never `strokeBorder` with a `Theme` colour (`make lint`). Both read `displayScale` and snap the width and the inset under it to whole device pixels through `Theme.inkWidth` and `Theme.underInk`, so 1.5 pt ink is 3 pixels on Retina and 2 on a 1x display. Corners are always `style: .circular` (`make lint`).
- The other shared controls are `ThemeToggleStyle` and `ThemeCheckboxStyle`, `ThemeSegmented`, `ThemePopUp` for a choice and `ThemeDropdown` for a menu of commands, and `ThemeSlider`. `ThemeFeedback.swift` has the text field (`.themeField(focused:)`), `ThemeSearchField`, `TagChip`, `ThemeBadge`, `Keycap`, `ThemeProgressBar` and `EmptyState`. Tooltips are `themeTip(_:key:detail:)` in `ThemeTip.swift`. `ButtonRow` puts a card's buttons side by side and stacks them, with the main action on top, when a translation or a larger text size doesn't fit. Settings rows are `SettingsCard`, `SettingRow` and `ToggleRow`, at the end of `ThemeStyle.swift`.
- `GlassWindow.make` in `GlassWindow.swift` makes a standard titled window with a unified, transparent title bar, its toolbar content beside the standard traffic lights, on the plain `Theme.window` background. `SidebarMaterial` is the see-through sidebar, solid with Increase Contrast or Reduce Transparency, and Settings and the gallery run it the window's full height. The editors' side panels draw their surface with `sidePanel(width:)` (`SidePanel.swift`), and `PanelResizeHandle` resizes a sidebar or panel by dragging its edge.
- A floating panel (the capture bar, recording controls, the countdown, a toast, Quick Access's buttons or the pin bar) is dark in both appearances. It's a `FloatingPanelWindow` sized to its shape, with `.floatingPanel(shape, opacity:)` on its content, `ThemeButtonStyle(…, floating: true)` on its buttons and `FloatingDivider` between groups of them. `FloatingPanel` holds the fill, the edge and the `Opacity` levels. `.floatingPanel` lays its content out in the dark appearance, so the quiet components inside pick up their dark values. The panel uses the system window shadow, not a drawn one, because a drawn shadow needs a transparent margin that catches clicks. The capture bar is the exception: AppKit draws it inside the overlay, so it draws `FloatingPanel.shadowNS` itself.
- Motion comes from `Motion` in `Design/Motion.swift`, which turns each ShotCore `Moment` (its length, and what Reduce Motion plays instead) into an animation. Use `Motion.animation(_:)` for a moment, `Motion.arrive` and `Motion.leave` for a panel coming and going, and `Motion.arriveCurve` for an AppKit window. Don't write a duration or spring by hand. ShotCore's tests keep every moment except the ambient loops under 300 ms, and Reduce Motion turns each into a 120 ms fade or nothing. A moment plays only after its work is done, so start it after the copy or save, never before. Edits and drags are always instant.
- A moment drawn over a whole screen, like `ViewfinderSnap`, uses Core Animation shape layers, not a SwiftUI view. A transparent screen-sized SwiftUI view paints a screen-sized bitmap, and that cost 6 MB in `make perf-app`.
- Views in lazy collections (`LazyVGrid`, `List`, `LazyVStack`): a row or tile reads only its own state. It takes plain values, not the shared `@Observable` model, for anything that changes often (selection, hover, the playing item), and is `Equatable` with `.equatable()`, so one change re-evaluates the few views whose values changed, not all of them.
- Put footers and status text that read such state in their own `View` structs, not computed properties of a big body.
- One interaction must cost the same however big the library is.
- Use a single `onTapGesture` and read `NSApp.currentEvent?.clickCount`. `onTapGesture(count: 2)` delays every single click (lint rejects it).
- UI and model types are `@MainActor`. Heavy work runs in `Task.detached`, and CG types cross actors through `@unchecked Sendable` wrappers (`SendableImage`, `EditorDocument`).
